# Oracle Price

The oracle price is the reference price used for funding calculations and as a stability anchor for the mark. It is published approximately every 3 seconds. Its construction depends on whether the underlying's primary venue is in its regular session.

### Time-Decay Convention

All exponential moving averages used in the spec are continuous-time. For an EMA state $$X$$ with half-life $$\tau$$, the update on a sample at time $$t\_k$$ is:

<p align="center"><span class="math">X(t_k) = e^{-\lambda \Delta t} X(t_{k-1}) + \left(1 - e^{-\lambda \Delta t}\right) X_{\mathrm{raw}}(t_k), \qquad \lambda = \frac{\ln 2}{\tau}, \qquad \Delta t = t_k - t_{k-1}</span></p>

This handles irregular sampling intervals correctly without requiring a fixed tick cadence.

### Internal Prices

Let the exchange mid price be:

<p align="center"><span class="math">P_{\mathrm{mid}}(t) = \frac{p_{\mathrm{bid}}(t) + p_{\mathrm{ask}}(t)}{2}</span></p>

The internal price is a continuous-time EMA of the mid with a 5-minute half-life, and the fast internal price is the analogous EMA with a 3-minute half-life:

<p align="center"><span class="math">I(t) = \operatorname{EMA}_{5\mathrm{min}}\!\left(P_{\mathrm{mid}}\right), \qquad I_3(t) = \operatorname{EMA}_{3\mathrm{min}}\!\left(P_{\mathrm{mid}}\right)</span></p>

The fast internal price is used in the market-hours mark. Dragging the mid requires exposing resting quotes away from fair value, so the economic cost of displacing the internal price scales with the size of the displacement.

### Public Reference Price

During market hours, the public reference price $$P\_{\mathrm{pub}}(t)$$ is the price observed on the underlying's primary public listing venue, sampled on each oracle tick. The last such observation of the session defines the last tick $$P\_{\mathrm{last}}$$, which is carried into the outside-hours regime.

### External Price

Outside market hours, the external price entering the oracle depends on whether the underlying has an approved after-hours venue.

If there is no approved after-hours venue, the external price is constant and equal to the last tick:

<p align="center"><span class="math">E(t) = P_{\mathrm{last}}</span></p>

If the underlying trades on an approved after-hours venue, the external price is that venue's latest valid mid price:

<p align="center"><span class="math">P_{\mathrm{mid}}^{\mathrm{ext}}(t) = \frac{p_{\mathrm{bid}}^{\mathrm{ext}}(t) + p_{\mathrm{ask}}^{\mathrm{ext}}(t)}{2}, \qquad E(t) = P_{\mathrm{mid}}^{\mathrm{ext}}(t)</span></p>

Here, $$p\_{\mathrm{bid}}^{\mathrm{ext}}(t)$$ and $$p\_{\mathrm{ask}}^{\mathrm{ext}}(t)$$ are the latest valid best bid and best ask from the approved venue. If either quote is unavailable, or the venue feed is stale, $$E(t)$$ falls back to $$P\_{\mathrm{last}}$$. The external price is not defined and not used during market hours.

### Depth-Gated Blend Weight

The outside-hours blend weight is driven by executable depth: each resting order's notional is scaled by an expected fill ratio before it enters the depth term, so orders resting far from the mid contribute less than orders that are likely to trade. For each resting order $$o$$ in the window, define its distance from the mid in basis points:

<p align="center"><span class="math">d_o(t) = 10{,}000 \cdot \frac{\left|p_o - P_{\mathrm{mid}}(t)\right|}{P_{\mathrm{mid}}(t)}</span></p>

The expected fill ratio is the distance-only logit:

<p align="center"><span class="math">\rho_o(t) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\!\left(0.15\, d_o(t)\right)}</span></p>

This is a heuristic conversion from posted notional to approximate expected filled notional; it intentionally omits queue position, volatility, and flow variables.

Executable depth is gated by the smaller of the two-sided sums inside a fixed 200 bps window around the mid. With $$\delta = 0.02$$:

<p align="center"><span class="math">D^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mathrm{ask}}(t) = \sum_{\substack{o \in \mathrm{ask} \\ p_o \in [P_{\mathrm{mid}}(t),\, P_{\mathrm{mid}}(t)(1+\delta)]}} p_o\, q_o\, \rho_o(t), \qquad D^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mathrm{bid}}(t) = \sum_{\substack{o \in \mathrm{bid} \\ p_o \in [P_{\mathrm{mid}}(t)(1-\delta),\, P_{\mathrm{mid}}(t)]}} p_o\, q_o\, \rho_o(t)</span></p>

<p align="center"><span class="math">D_{\min}(t) = \min\!\left(D^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mathrm{ask}}(t),\; D^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mathrm{bid}}(t)\right)</span></p>

The depth signal uses the same 5-minute half-life as the internal price:

<p align="center"><span class="math">x_D(t) = \min\!\left(\operatorname{EMA}_{5\mathrm{min}}(D_{\min}),\; D_{\min}(t)\right)</span></p>

The internal weight is generated by the liquidity curve:

<p align="center"><span class="math">C_{\mathrm{liq}}(x) = \min\!\left(\frac{\log_b\!\left(1 + \dfrac{x\, b}{a}\right)}{\log_b(1 + b)},\; 0.95\right), \qquad b = 1000</span></p>

<p align="center"><span class="math">w_I(t) = C_{\mathrm{liq}}\!\left(x_D(t)\right)</span></p>

The scale parameter $$a$$ is calibrated in expected-filled-notional units and is subject to discretionary revision as venue liquidity grows. Per-market values are listed under Assets. The ceiling ensures the external side always retains at least 5% weight.

### Oracle and Mark by Regime

**Market hours.** The oracle is pinned to the public reference price, and the mark is the mean of the public reference price and the fast internal price:

<p align="center"><span class="math">O(t) = P_{\mathrm{pub}}(t), \qquad M(t) = \frac{P_{\mathrm{pub}}(t) + I_3(t)}{2}</span></p>

Averaging the public reference against the 3-minute EMA of the mid lets the mark reflect local order flow while remaining anchored to the public venue; neither leg alone can move the mark by more than half of its own displacement.

**Outside market hours.** The oracle is the liquidity-weighted blend of the internal and external prices, and the mark is the internal price clipped to leverage-parameterized bounds centered on the last tick:

<p align="center"><span class="math">O(t) = w_I(t)\, I(t) + \left(1 - w_I(t)\right) E(t)</span></p>

<p align="center"><span class="math">L = \left(1 - \frac{1}{\ell_{max}}\right) P_{\mathrm{last}}, \qquad U = \left(1 + \frac{1}{\ell_{max}}\right) P_{\mathrm{last}}, \qquad M(t) = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(I(t);\, L,\, U\right)</span></p>

Only the mark is subject to the outside-hours bounds; the oracle remains the unclipped blend. Because the outside-hours bound sits one initial-margin fraction $$1/\ell\_{max}$$ above and below the last public print, a closed-hours excursion cannot move the mark past the point where a maximum-leverage position is covered by its own posted collateral. This is what produces limit-up / limit-down behavior on the mark while the underlying's public market is closed.

### Session Transitions

At the market-hours close, $$P\_{\mathrm{last}}$$ is set to the final market-hours oracle tick and the outside-hours regime begins immediately. At the open, the oracle switches to $$P\_{\mathrm{pub}}(t)$$ on the first tick with an observable public reference price and the outside-hours bounds are disabled. The EMA states run continuously across session boundaries and are not reset. If the public feed becomes unavailable or stale during market hours, the market falls back to the outside-hours regime, treating the last good observation as $$P\_{\mathrm{last}}$$, until the feed recovers.
